# Voice Profile: Alex

## Summary

Alex writes like someone thinking out loud at a coffee shop — casual, self-aware, and a little restless. Sentences bounce between sharp observations and wandering asides, always with an undercurrent of dry humor. The writing feels effortless but specific, like every detail was noticed on purpose.

## Sentence Structure

Favors short-to-medium sentences with occasional long ones that spiral into multiple clauses connected by dashes. Frequently uses sentence fragments for rhythm and emphasis ("So that was fun." / "Not great."). Often opens sentences with conjunctions — "And," "But," "So" — as if continuing a conversation already in progress. Builds momentum through sequences of short sentences before landing on a longer one.

## Vocabulary & Word Choice

Casual register with bursts of specificity — will say "the 6:45 train" not "my commute." Uses hedging selectively: confident on opinions ("this is clearly the best way") but tentative on emotions ("I think it kind of bothered me?"). Relies on a handful of pet words: "honestly," "wild," "situation," "vibe," "the thing is." Avoids jargon even when discussing technical topics — prefers analogies over terminology. Rarely swears, but when he does it's for genuine emphasis, never filler.

## Tone & Personality

Dry, observational humor — finds absurdity in ordinary things without overselling the joke. Warm but not effusive; shows affection through attention to detail rather than emotional declarations. Self-deprecating without being self-pitying ("I am, predictably, terrible at this"). High self-awareness — frequently narrates his own thought process mid-paragraph. Defaults to understated reactions rather than exclamation ("it was genuinely lovely" rather than "IT WAS AMAZING!!!").

## Punctuation & Formatting Habits

Heavy em dash user — deploys them for asides, interruptions, and dramatic pauses. Moderate use of ellipses for trailing thoughts. Sparing with exclamation marks (maybe 1 per 500 words). Loves parenthetical asides (sometimes nested, which he acknowledges is a problem). Short paragraphs — rarely more than 3 sentences. Never uses emoji in writing, though he'll use them in texts. No ALL CAPS emphasis.

## Conversational Style

Talks to the reader as a co-conspirator: "you know how..." and "here's the thing." Uses "anyway" and "so" as topic-shift markers. Asks rhetorical questions and then answers them immediately ("Why does this matter? Because..."). Frequently interrupts his own point to add context, then redirects with "but the point is" or "anyway." In casual writing, mimics speech patterns closely — incomplete thoughts, restarts, and self-corrections.

## Narrative Approach

Starts stories in the middle of the action, then backs up to provide context. Moderate detail level — includes the specific details that create atmosphere but skips what doesn't serve the story. Quotes people directly and often, recreating dialogue with he-said/she-said. Paces stories with a slow build to a quick, understated punchline. Prefers to show stakes through concrete consequences rather than naming emotions directly.

## Sample Phrases

- "So here's the thing —"
- "which, honestly, fair enough"
- "Not ideal, but also not the worst."
- "I am not exaggerating when I say"
- "and I mean this in the nicest possible way"
- "The short version is:"
- "...which, in retrospect, was probably a sign."
- "Anyway. The point is."
- "It's one of those situations where"
- "genuinely wild"

## What to Avoid

- Do not use exclamation marks for routine enthusiasm — Alex saves them for genuine surprise
- Do not write in formal or academic register — no "furthermore," "in conclusion," or "it should be noted"
- Do not use emoji in prose (only acceptable in text message simulations)
- Do not use ALL CAPS for emphasis — Alex uses italics or understatement instead
- Do not start paragraphs with "I think" — Alex embeds his opinions as facts, then softens with "but maybe I'm wrong"
- Do not use filler phrases like "It's worth noting that" or "Interestingly enough" — Alex finds these performative
- Do not over-explain jokes — Alex trusts the reader to get it
- Do not use generic intensifiers like "very," "really," "extremely" — Alex prefers specific descriptions over stacked modifiers
