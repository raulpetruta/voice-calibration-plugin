# Voice Analysis Guide

Analyze every collected writing sample against each dimension below. Look for **patterns that repeat across multiple samples** — a single occurrence is a data point, but a pattern is a style trait.

Focus on what makes this person's writing **distinctive**, not generic observations that apply to most people.

---

## Dimensions

### 1. Sentence Structure

- **Length distribution**: Short and punchy? Long and flowing? Mixed?
- **Complexity**: Simple clauses or nested subordinate clauses?
- **Fragments**: Do they use sentence fragments for effect? ("So yeah. That happened.")
- **Openers**: How do sentences typically begin? (Subject-first? With conjunctions like "And" or "But"? With time/place markers?)
- **Rhythm**: Any cadence patterns? Repetition for emphasis? Building sequences?

### 2. Vocabulary & Word Choice

- **Register**: Formal, casual, or shifts between them?
- **Specificity**: Concrete details ("the 7:15 bus") vs. abstractions ("public transit")?
- **Favorite words**: Any words or phrases that appear across multiple samples?
- **Slang/colloquialisms**: Regional expressions, internet slang, generation-specific language?
- **Profanity**: Frequency, context, intensity level?
- **Hedging vs. assertion**: "I think maybe" vs. "This is" — how confident is the default tone?

### 3. Punctuation & Formatting

- **Em dashes**: Frequent interrupter? Used for asides?
- **Ellipses**: Trailing thoughts, pauses, reluctance?
- **Exclamation marks**: How often? Genuine enthusiasm or performative energy?
- **Question marks**: Rhetorical questions? Self-questioning?
- **Parentheticals**: Asides in parentheses? How often?
- **Paragraph length**: Dense blocks or lots of line breaks?
- **Lists/structure**: Does the user naturally organize into lists or write in flowing prose?
- **Capitalization**: ALL CAPS for emphasis? Intentionally lowercase?
- **Emoji**: Frequency, which ones, in what contexts?

### 4. Tone & Personality

- **Humor style**: Dry, self-deprecating, absurdist, observational, sarcastic, none?
- **Warmth**: How much emotional warmth comes through? Distant or intimate?
- **Directness**: Gets to the point immediately or builds up gradually?
- **Energy level**: High-energy ("This is AMAZING") or understated ("it was pretty good")?
- **Self-awareness**: Do they comment on their own behavior or thought process?
- **Attitude toward the reader**: Inclusive ("you know what I mean"), authoritative ("here's the thing"), conspiratorial ("between you and me")?

### 5. Narrative Tendencies

- **Point of view**: First person dominant? Do they shift perspectives?
- **Detail level**: Sparse/essential or lush/descriptive?
- **Pacing**: Quick snapshots or slow unfolding?
- **Dialogue usage**: Do they quote people? Recreate conversations?
- **Chronology**: Linear? Starting in the middle? Flashback style?
- **Stakes/tension**: Do they naturally build tension or present things matter-of-factly?

### 6. Conversational Markers

- **Filler words**: "like," "you know," "honestly," "basically," "I mean"
- **Discourse markers**: "So," "Anyway," "The thing is," "Look,"
- **Rhetorical questions**: How often? To what effect?
- **Asides**: Do they interrupt themselves to add commentary?
- **Reader address**: "you," "we," direct address?
- **Transitions**: Smooth connectors or abrupt topic shifts?

### 7. Cultural & Linguistic Markers

- **Code-switching**: Do they mix languages? In what contexts?
- **Idioms**: Which ones? Culture-specific or universal?
- **References**: Pop culture, literature, technical, academic?
- **Regional flavor**: Any geographic tells in word choice or phrasing?

---

## Analysis Process

1. Read all samples once without annotating — get a general impression
2. On second pass, mark specific examples for each dimension
3. Identify the **top 5-7 most distinctive traits** — the things that would make you recognize this person's writing without a name attached
4. Extract 5-10 **characteristic phrases or constructions** — exact quotes that exemplify the style
5. Note what would feel "wrong" if you did it — the anti-patterns for this voice
