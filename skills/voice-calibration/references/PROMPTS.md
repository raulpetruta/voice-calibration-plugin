# Writing Prompts Bank

Select prompts from at least 4 categories. Always include at least one from **Casual Conversation** and one from **Opinions & Arguments**.

---

## 1. Micro-Stories

Short narrative prompts that reveal storytelling instincts, pacing, and detail preferences.

1. **The pocket**: Write a 3-5 sentence story about finding something unexpected in your pocket.
2. **The stranger**: Someone sits next to you on a bus and says something weird. What happens next? (4-6 sentences)
3. **The sound**: You hear a sound in the middle of the night that you can't explain. Write what happens.
4. **Last day**: Write about the last hour of the last day of school/work before a long vacation.

## 2. Opinions & Arguments

Reveal persuasion style, conviction level, and how the user builds a case.

1. **Food pitch**: Convince a skeptical friend to try your favorite food or restaurant.
2. **Unpopular take**: Share an opinion you hold that most people around you disagree with. Defend it.
3. **Recommendation**: A friend asks you to recommend a book, show, or game. Write your pitch.
4. **The debate**: You overhear someone say something you strongly disagree with. How do you respond (or do you)?

## 3. Casual Conversation

Captures natural, unguarded communication style — where personality shows most.

1. **The text**: Text a friend about something funny or weird that happened to you today.
2. **The voice memo**: You're leaving a voice memo for a friend explaining why you're running late. Write it out.
3. **The rant**: Something mildly annoying happened. Rant about it to a close friend.
4. **The update**: A friend you haven't talked to in months asks "what's new?" Write your response.

## 4. Descriptions & Observations

Reveals sensory awareness, metaphor use, and attention to detail.

1. **Morning routine**: Describe your morning routine as if narrating a documentary about yourself.
2. **The place**: Describe a place that feels like home to you. Not necessarily your house.
3. **The person**: Describe someone you know well — how they move, talk, their habits — without naming them.
4. **Right now**: Describe exactly what you see around you right now, in your own words.

## 5. Emotional Range

Shows how the user handles vulnerability, enthusiasm, nostalgia, and intensity.

1. **The surprise**: Write about a time you were genuinely surprised — good or bad.
2. **The thing you love**: Write about something you love doing. Why does it matter to you?
3. **The memory**: Describe a vivid memory from childhood. What makes it stick?
4. **The frustration**: Write about a time you tried really hard at something and it didn't work out.

## 6. Technical & Explanatory

Captures how the user teaches, simplifies, and structures information.

1. **Explain it**: Pick something you know well and explain it to someone who knows nothing about it.
2. **The process**: Walk someone through a process you do regularly — cooking a dish, debugging code, planning a trip.
3. **Why it works**: Pick a tool, app, or system you love and explain what makes it good.
4. **The analogy**: Explain a complex concept using an analogy or metaphor that you'd actually use.
